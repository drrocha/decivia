How it works

The liquid's cross-section at a given fill height is a circular segment, not a simple percentage of the circle:

Segment area = ½r²(θ − sin θ), where θ = 2·cos⁻¹((r − h) / r)

Here r is the tank radius and h is the fill height measured from the bottom. The segment area × tank length ÷ 231 gives the fill volume in US gallons.

Worked example

A 24 in diameter × 60 in long tank filled to 12 in (exactly half): the segment area equals half the circle's area (π × 12² ÷ 2 ≈ 226 in²), giving 226 × 60 ÷ 231 ≈ 58.7 gallons — exactly half of the tank's ~117.5 gallon total capacity, as expected at the midpoint.

Assumptions

  • Tank ends are flat (not domed/elliptical); domed ends add extra volume not modeled here.
  • Fill height is measured vertically from the lowest inside point of the tank.
  • Waste space such as sump depth or an outlet above the floor is not subtracted automatically.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate the volume of a horizontal cylindrical tank at partial fill?

Find the circular-segment area at the fill height, then multiply by the tank length. It is not a simple linear percentage of total volume.

Does fill percentage mean 50% height is 50% volume?

Only at exactly the halfway point. Other fill heights are not a straight-line percentage because of the circular cross-section.

How do I measure fill height on a real tank?

Measure the vertical distance from the bottom inside of the tank to the liquid surface, using a dipstick or sight gauge.
