How it works

Rectangular tank volume is length × width × height. Vertical cylinder volume is π × radius² × height. In Imperial units, cubic inches convert to US gallons by dividing by 231 (1 US gallon = 231 in³). In Metric units, cubic centimeters convert to liters by dividing by 1,000 (1,000 cm³ = 1 L).

Worked example

Imperial: A rectangular tank 48 in × 24 in × 24 in holds 27,648 in³, which equals 119.7 gallons (16.0 cu ft).
Metric equivalent: 121.9 cm × 61.0 cm × 61.0 cm holds 453,592 cm³, which equals 453.6 liters (0.45 m³).

Assumptions

  • Tank walls are thin enough that inside dimensions ≈ outside dimensions; use inside dimensions for best accuracy.
  • Volume is total/nominal capacity — real-world usable volume is often slightly less due to fittings or an outlet above the floor.

Frequently asked questions

How many gallons are in a cylindrical tank?

Multiply the cross-sectional area (π × radius²) by the height, then divide by 231 for US gallons.

How do I calculate the volume of a rectangular tank?

Multiply length × width × height for cubic inches, then divide by 231 for US gallons.

What if my tank is a horizontal cylinder?

Use the fuel tank capacity calculator, which supports horizontal cylinders, or the fill-level calculator for partial-fill volume.
