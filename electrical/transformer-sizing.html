How Transformer VA is Calculated

Apparent power represents the total volt-ampere demand on the supply:

Single-Phase: VA = Voltage × Current

Three-Phase: VA = Voltage × Current × √3 (≈ 1.732)

Worked Example

For a single-phase 240 V, 20 A circuit: VA = 240 × 20 = 4,800 VA (4.8 kVA). The next standard NEMA size is 5,000 VA (5 kVA). If sized for continuous duty (+25% margin): 4,800 × 1.25 = 6,000 VA, leading to a 7,500 VA (7.5 kVA) transformer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I size a transformer to match the exact calculated VA?

No. Industry best practice recommends adding a 20% to 25% safety margin for continuous duty and future expansion. Transformers operating at near 100% capacity continuously produce excess heat and experience shorter lifespans.

What is the difference between VA and Watts?

Watts measure real power consumed by resistive loads. Volt-Amperes (VA) measure apparent power including reactive current caused by inductors and capacitors (power factor). Transformer coils and wiring must be sized to carry total VA.
